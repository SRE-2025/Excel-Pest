Central Texas Pest Calendar: A Month-by-Month Checklist

Twelve practical check-ins for your home and yard, with links to current Excel Pest services. Local weather can shift pest activity throughout the year.

Pest Guide · Published Oct 23, 2026

Quick answer: Check your home throughout the year: entry points and storage in cooler months, outdoor water and ant activity as conditions warm, and lawn damage and hiding places in summer. Weather matters more than exact dates.

Use this Central Texas pest calendar as a home-maintenance checklist, not a prediction that a pest will appear in a particular month. Rain, temperature, irrigation and a property's condition can change activity. Indoor problems can persist all year.

Start with the tasks that match your home in Buda, Kyle, San Marcos, Dripping Springs or Wimberley.

January: Check stored items and rodent access

Inspect accessible garage storage and look for fresh gnawing or droppings. Record activity rather than entering an unsafe attic.

May: Revisit the yard after rain

Look for active ant mounds and new standing water. Keep children and pets away from mounds; ask about a property-specific treatment plan.

July: Look closely at damaged turf

September: Review food, moisture and storage

October: Plan for attic and garage prevention

Walk the exterior from ground level and look for damaged vents or roofline openings. Repeated noises are a reason to request a rodent assessment.

November: Check decorations before bringing them inside

Inspect accessible storage containers and keep pet food secured. Report fresh animal activity or entry damage without approaching wildlife.

December: Review the year's recurring problems

Compare notes on sightings and previous visits. Ask which preventive tasks and service intervals fit your home for the coming year.

Three habits for every month

  • Record the location, timing and frequency of pest activity.
  • Check accessible door gaps, screens, leaks and outdoor water containers.
  • Review recurring problems with the technician instead of assuming every sighting needs the same treatment.
